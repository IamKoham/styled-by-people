# Regulatory and Platform-Policy Landscape for a Gen Z Fashion Styling Game (as of Nov 2026)

Scope: a styling game with IAP (coin packs, passes), affiliate links to real clothing, UGC looks with player voting, profiles, aggregated trend data sold to brands, and rewards for sign-up, social sharing (TikTok/IG/FB) and referrals. US primary; UK/EU secondary. NOT LEGAL ADVICE. Items marked [lawyer] need counsel review. Items marked [background, not re-verified this session] come from well-established public record but were not freshly fetched during this research pass; the writer should treat them as reliable context but confirm before quoting specifics.

## 1. Minors' privacy and safety law (US federal and state, UK, EU)

### Takeaway
Under-13 is the hard COPPA line (amended rule fully in force since April 22, 2026), but the 2025-2026 wave of state law is shifting the real burden to the 13-17 band: app-store age-signal laws (Texas now enforceable; Utah, Louisiana, Alabama, California due by 2027), a partly-revived California AADC, and social-media parental-consent laws (Florida HB 3 back in effect) mean a 16+ app still has minors and still gets age signals it must act on. Only a genuinely enforced 18+ largely exits these regimes, and even then app-store age signals and "actual knowledge" can pull minors back in.

### Inferences
- A 16+ US launch is outside COPPA (unless actual knowledge of under-13s), but it is squarely inside CAADCA (under-18), app-store age-signal laws (under-18 categories), and possibly Florida HB 3-type laws only if classed as social media (HB 3 targets under-16, so a 16+ floor likely avoids it).
- App-store laws convert "we said 16+" into "we received a signal this user is 13-15 / 16-17", creating actual knowledge. Product must branch on store age signals.
- Trend in 2026 appellate courts (5th, 6th, 11th Circuits) is toward upholding age-verification/parental-consent laws, so planning should assume these laws stick.

## 2. In-app purchases and minors

### Takeaway
US: FTC treats confusing multi-currency flows and frictionless charges to kids/teens as unfair practices (Epic $245M; Genshin $20M with an under-16 parental-consent purchase requirement and a ban on loot boxes sold only via virtual currency). EU: the CPC network's 2025 principles already expect real-money price display, and the Commission's Nov 2026 Digital Fairness Act proposal would ban paid loot boxes and pay-to-win for under-18s and require paid currencies off-by-default for minors. A styling game should avoid randomized paid items entirely and show real-money equivalents.

### Cited Findings
- Epic Games (Dec 2022): two FTC cases — COPPA (penalty) and dark patterns ($245M refunds) for tricking players into purchases and making refunds/cancellation hard; order requires express informed consent before charging for in-game purchases. Total ~$520M. — [Kelley Drye](https://kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/podcasts/ad-law-access/two-epic-cases-from-the-ftc); [WJLA](https://wjla.com/news/nation-world/fortnite-to-pay-520m-in-penalties-refunds-for-privacy-e-commerce-abuses-epic-games-federal-trade-commission-unintended-purchases-regulations-dark-patterns-apple-iphone-apps); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/15/ftc-fortnite-epic-games-purchases-settlement/amp/)
- Cognosphere/HoYoverse (Genshin Impact, Jan 2025): $20M; must block under-16s from in-game purchases without parental consent; FTC cited "complicated and confusing" transactions with multiple virtual currencies and exchange rates; barred from selling loot boxes via virtual currency without a direct real-money option. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/ftc-says-genshin-impact-deceived-children-and-orders-its-publisher-to-pay-a-usd20-million-fine-and-stop-selling-loot-boxes-to-kids); [Claims Journal](https://www.claimsjournal.com/news/national/2025/01/22/328527.htm)
- Note: Genshin's under-16 consent requirement shows the FTC reaching teens 13-15 via unfairness, not just COPPA's under-13. — same sources
- EU CPC Network Key Principles on in-game virtual currencies (Mar 21, 2025; non-binding): show prices in real money; avoid multiple currencies/forced exchanges that obscure cost; don't force buying more currency than needed (bundle sizing); gameplay-only currencies excluded. — [Linklaters](https://techinsights.linklaters.com/post/102k6t4/game-changer-eu-introduces-consumer-protection-guidance-for-in-game-virtual-curr); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/european-consumer-protection-network-issues-new-key-principles-on-in-game-virtual-currencies-impact-for-gaming-and-gambling-entities-in-belgium-the-eu-and-beyond/); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2025/european-move-towards-protecting-users-from-harmful-practices-in-video-games-eu-consumer-protection)
- Industry (EGDF, Video Games Europe) criticized the process; CPC says following principles doesn't guarantee compliance. — [Reed Smith](https://www.reedsmith.com/articles/qas-on-the-eu-consumer-protection-authorities-joint-guidance-paper/)
- EU Digital Fairness Act (proposed Nov 11, 2026; PENDING, not law): Art. 14 real-money value display for virtual-currency purchases; Art. 15 loot box odds/item disclosure and off-switch for all players, ban on paid loot boxes for minors; Art. 16 extends minors ban to pay-to-progress/pay-to-win; paid currencies "disabled by default" in games aimed at minors or where provider is "aware with reasonable certainty" minors play (parental opt-in); Art. 17 lets players disable compulsive-use features (e.g., login streak penalties, non-action notifications). Applies 12 months after entry into force; goes to Parliament and Council. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28479/european-commission-proposes-ban-on-paid-loot-boxes-and-pay-to-win-for-minors); [Gaming.net](https://www.gaming.net/european-commission-proposes-loot-box-and-virtual-currency-rules/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/eu-digital-fairness-act-loot-boxes-dark-patterns)
- Reporting conflict: early coverage relied on a leaked draft; outlets differ on Nov 10 vs Nov 11 date and on whether minors' currency lock is parental-approval or flat. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28479/european-commission-proposes-ban-on-paid-loot-boxes-and-pay-to-win-for-minors); [GodisaGeek](https://godisageek.com/2026/11/eu-loot-box-pay-to-win-ban-minors/)
- Loot boxes elsewhere [background, not re-verified this session]: Belgium Gaming Commission (2018) treats paid loot boxes as illegal gambling; Netherlands Council of State (2022) overturned the EA FIFA fine, so Dutch status is effectively permissive pending legislation; UK government (2022) declined to legislate, relying on industry principles (Ukie, 2023) incl. no paid random items for under-18s without parental consent and probability disclosure.
- App-store parental controls (Apple Ask to Buy / Screen Time; Google Play Family Link purchase approval) are the default purchase-approval mechanism for minor accounts; app-store age laws (Texas, Utah) also require store-mediated parental consent for minors' purchases. [background; lawyer to confirm developer obligations under each state act]

### Inferences
- For a styling game: no randomized paid items (no "mystery outfit boxes" for coins bought with money); show real-money price next to coin prices; size coin packs to match item prices; one paid currency only; clear pass terms/auto-renew disclosures; easy refunds.
- Streak/login-reward mechanics with loss penalties are a DFA Art. 17 target and fit FTC dark-pattern theories — design them as non-punitive.
- Teen mode would need purchase controls akin to Genshin order (parental consent for under-16) and spend caps.

- No 2026 FTC game IAP enforcement action found in this pass.
- Current status of UK loot box industry-principles review and any Dutch/Belgian 2026 updates not verified.

## 3. Affiliate/shopping links and advertising to minors

### Takeaway
Affiliate links require clear, proximate "we earn a commission" disclosure under FTC law for all ages; for under-18 users, CARU (US self-regulatory, applies to under-13 and its guidance on blurring ads/content) and UK CAP code (under-16 "children") add stricter rules against direct exhortation to buy and blurred commercial content. An 18+ launch largely avoids child-advertising codes; 16+ in the UK escapes CAP's "children" definition but not general teen-protection principles.

### Cited Findings
- FTC Endorsement Guides (revised June 2023) require clear and conspicuous disclosure of material connections; affiliate relationships are material connections. Revisions added an explicit concern regarding child-directed advertising. [background, not re-verified this session] — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements)
- CARU Advertising Guidelines (revised 2022, effective 2024) apply to advertising directed to children under 13 and address in-app/in-game ads, blurring, and pressure to purchase. [background, not re-verified this session] — [BBB National Programs CARU](https://bbbprograms.org/programs/all-programs/caru)
- UK CAP Code section 5 (children = under 16): no direct exhortation to children to buy or to ask parents; ads must be obviously identifiable. [background, not re-verified this session] — [ASA/CAP](https://www.asa.org.uk/type/non_broadcast/code_section/05.html)
- EU DSA Art. 28(2) bans profiling-based ads to minors on online platforms when the provider is reasonably certain the user is a minor. [background, not re-verified this session] — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors)
- Amended COPPA requires separate parental consent to disclose under-13 data for targeted advertising — affiliate tracking that shares identifiers with retailers would trigger this for any under-13s. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)

### Inferences
- Label affiliate items ("Shop - we may earn a commission") on every shoppable card, not only in ToS.
- Do not award coins for clicking/buying affiliate items (blurs incentive and endorsement; for minors it is a direct-exhortation risk).
- For teen mode: disable personalized affiliate recommendations, consider no affiliate links or only non-tracked links, and avoid age-restricted products. [lawyer]

### Gaps
- Did not find any US law specifically prohibiting directing 13-17s to shopping links; restrictions are via unfairness, CARU (under-13), state design codes and EU DSA profiling ban.
- Current CAP code and CARU text not freshly verified.

## 4. Incentivized sharing and referrals

### Takeaway
Rewarding users for posting to Facebook is prohibited by Meta's developer policy; rewarding referrals that result in installs is allowed. Apple prohibits gating features behind sharing/reviews and the store considers rewarding new users for installing to be problematic, while rewarding the referrer is accepted. Google bars incentivized ratings/reviews/installs. FTC: rewarded posts are endorsements with a material connection and need disclosure; incentivized reviews conditioned on sentiment are illegal under the 2024 Consumer Reviews Rule. Compliant pattern: reward successful referrals (not posts), never reward reviews, add auto-disclosure tags to shared content.

### Cited Findings
- Meta developer policy: don't incentivize people to post content to Facebook or give the impression posting will be rewarded; no in-game points per friend shared with; referral-based rewards (items/discounts per friend who installs via a post) are compliant. — [Meta Policies Deep Dive](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/apps/policies/policies-deep-dive/); [Meta Incentivization Deep Dive](https://developers.secure.facebook.com/docs/development/terms-and-policies/policy-deep-dive); [Meta Platform Terms](https://developers.facebook.com/terms)
- Meta promotion guidelines: promotions must not require or incentivize sharing, reposting, tagging or publicizing (incl. bonus entries). — [ShortStack summary](https://www.shortstack.com/help/facebook-promotion-guidelines/)
- Meta's content-distribution guidelines demote "inauthentic sharing". — [Meta Transparency](https://transparency.meta.com/features/approach-to-ranking/content-distribution-guidelines/inauthentic-sharing)
- Apple App Review Guideline 3.2.2(vi) (unacceptable business model): forcing users to rate, review, watch videos, download other apps, tap ads etc. to access functionality/content. — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/); [Apple news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=xqk627qu)
- Apple 2014 history: initially rejected rewarded sharing, then clarified rewards for social sharing allowed if not required and not used to manipulate rankings. — [AppleInsider](https://appleinsider.com/articles/14/06/24/apple-relaxes-clarifies-new-incentivized-sharing-rules-for-the-app-store/amp/)
- Developer-forum report of an Apple rejection: rewarding the inviter is acceptable but the new user should not get rewards for downloading/registering (anecdotal, not official). — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/732915)
- Apple guideline 1.1.6 / 5.6.2-type rules also prohibit manipulating ratings/reviews; incentivizing App Store reviews is barred. [background, not re-verified this session] — [Apple App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Google Play: incentivized installs, ratings and reviews (offering money, goods or equivalent) are treated as manipulation; incentivized installs filtered from charts. — [Android Developers Blog (2017)](https://android-developers.googleblog.com/2017/06/google-plays-policy-on-incentivized.html)
- FTC Consumer Reviews and Testimonials Rule (final Aug 2024) bans offering compensation conditioned on reviews expressing a particular sentiment. [background, not re-verified this session] — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials)
- FTC Endorsement Guides (2023): incentives to post create a material connection requiring disclosure; incentivized reviews must be disclosed. [background, not re-verified this session] — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/06/federal-trade-commission-announces-updated-advertising-guides-combat-deceptive-reviews-endorsements)
- TikTok: developers using TikTok Share Kit are governed by TikTok developer terms; TikTok's branded content policy requires a content disclosure toggle for posts made in exchange for value. [background, not re-verified this session; no fresh source found]

### Inferences
- Remove "earn coins for sharing to Facebook/Instagram" — violates Meta policy and requires FTC disclosure. Safer: share is free and unrewarded; reward only when a referred friend installs and completes onboarding (Meta-compliant; Apple-accepted when the inviter is rewarded).
- Sign-up rewards (welcome coins) are fine; don't condition them on sharing or rating.
- If any reward for posting remains (e.g., TikTok challenge), auto-insert #ad / "Made with [App], rewarded" and enable platform disclosure toggles; for minors, avoid entirely.
- Never reward App Store / Play reviews; use native rating prompts.

### Gaps

- Current Google Play Developer Program Policy text on referral rewards not retrieved (2017 blog only).

## 5. User-generated content and voting

### Takeaway
UGC + voting makes the game a user-to-user service under the UK OSA and a hosting/online platform under the EU DSA, with notice-and-action, reporting, and (if accessible to minors) children's risk assessments and safety-by-default. In the US, Section 230 protects moderation but state design codes and the FTC push anti-bullying, private-by-default teen settings, and no public ranking of minors' appearance.

### Cited Findings
- Ofcom children's codes require content moderation, reporting/complaints, safer recommender systems, age assurance and user tools for services likely accessed by children. — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/cy/online-safety/protecting-children/protection-of-children-duties-under-the-online-safety-act)
- Ofcom expects highly effective age assurance, not self-declaration, where services restrict minors. — [Ofcom (Mar 2026)](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/keep-underage-children-off-your-platforms-ofcom-tells-tech-firms)
- EU Art. 28 guidelines (accessible to minors): private-by-default accounts, safer recommender systems, reporting tools; benchmark for enforcement. — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/07/rd-european-commission-guidelines-on-protection-of-minors-under-the-digital-services-act); [A&L Goodbody PDF](https://www.algoodbody.com/files/uploads/news_insights_pub/Protection_of_minors_on_online_platforms_DSA_Guidelines.pdf)
- 55% of UK children who game online play with people they have never met in person (contact-risk relevance). — [PocketGamer.biz citing Ofcom](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/ofcom-warns-children-remain-exposed-to-online-safety-risks-across-platforms-including-roblox-tiktok-and-youtube/)
- CAADCA's non-enjoined provisions (e.g., DPIA-type risk assessment and default privacy settings) may now be enforceable. — [DLA Piper](https://privacymatters.dlapiper.com/2026/03/the-ninth-circuits-latest-caadca-ruling-navigating-an-evolving-compliance-landscape/) [lawyer to map which]

### Inferences
- Vote on looks, not people: avatar-only looks (no selfies/body photos), no public "hot or not" of real bodies, no downvotes or public low scores; free text comments heavily filtered or replaced by preset reactions. This lowers both bullying/body-image risk and OSA/DSA risk.
- Need: report button on every look/profile, block/mute, human review queue, image moderation (if uploads exist), and a documented risk assessment (UK OSA children's risk assessment if likely accessed by UK under-18s).

### Gaps
- No specific regulator guidance found on appearance-voting/ranking features for teens; inference based on general body-image/bullying expectations.

## 6. Selling aggregated user data to brands

### Takeaway
Truly de-identified/aggregated data falls outside CCPA "personal information," but cohort-level trend data that can be linked to devices or households can be "sale." For known 13-15s, CCPA requires opt-in consent to sell; under-13 requires parental opt-in. GDPR treats sharing with brands as needing a lawful basis and transparency; aggregated anonymous statistics are outside GDPR if truly anonymized. An 18+ launch removes the minors' opt-in layer but not general opt-out, notice, and data-broker considerations.

### Cited Findings
- CCPA §1798.140: "sale" includes selling, renting, disclosing or making available personal information to a third party for monetary or other valuable consideration; "deidentified" and "aggregate consumer information" are excluded from personal information if conditions met. [background, not re-verified this session] — [Cal. Civ. Code 1798.140](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?sectionNum=1798.140.&lawCode=CIV)
- CCPA §1798.120(c)-(d): businesses may not sell/share PI of consumers they actually know are under 16 without affirmative authorization (13-15 by the consumer; under 13 by parent); willful disregard of age = actual knowledge. [background, not re-verified this session] — [Cal. Civ. Code 1798.120](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=CIV&sectionNum=1798.120.)
- Several other state comprehensive privacy laws (e.g., Connecticut, Colorado, Oregon, Maryland) restrict sale/targeted advertising of known minors' (under 16/18) data without consent. [background, not re-verified this session; lawyer]
- Amended COPPA requires separate verifiable parental consent for third-party disclosures not integral to the service. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)
- GDPR Recital 26: anonymous data outside scope; pseudonymized data remains personal data. [background, not re-verified this session] — [GDPR Recital 26](https://gdpr-info.eu/recitals/no-26/)
- CAADCA data-use restrictions (§1798.99.31(b)(1)-(4)) remain enjoined as of March 2026, so California minors' data-use limits currently come chiefly from CCPA. — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-30-netchoice-v-bonta-ninth-circuit-narrows-injunction-against-californias-ageappropriate-design-code-act)

### Inferences
- Sell only aggregated, thresholded trend reports (minimum cohort sizes, no device IDs, no raw rows), contractually ban re-identification — keeps outside "sale" in most states.
- Exclude all users known/flagged as under 18 (including store age-signal flags) from any brand dataset — simplest way to neutralize minors' rules.
- Disclose trend-data program in privacy policy; honor opt-outs/GPC.

### Gaps
- No fresh source on 2026 state-law changes to minors' data sale (e.g., new under-18 sale bans).
- California Delete Act/data broker registration applicability to a first-party app selling aggregates not researched. [lawyer]

## 7. Practical compliance burden: 13+ vs 16+ vs 18+

### Takeaway
13+ is the heaviest: full minors' regime (UK/EU children's codes, CAADCA, store age-signal branching, Genshin-style teen purchase consent, CCPA opt-in for sale of 13-15 data, likely state social-media consent laws incl. Florida HB 3 for 14-15, EU GDPR parental consent in 16-age countries). 16+ removes CCPA under-16 opt-in, Florida HB 3, CAP "children," most GDPR Art. 8 parental-consent, and Genshin under-16 consent, but keeps under-18 regimes (CAADCA, UK OSA/Children's Code, EU DSA Art. 28 and proposed DFA under-18 bans, app-store minor categories). 18+ removes most minor-specific design duties, but only if age assurance is credible enough to satisfy Ofcom/EU (self-declaration is considered insufficient), and app-store age signals can still create actual knowledge of minors.

### Cited Findings
- COPPA applies to under-13 child-directed services or actual knowledge. — [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/coppas-amended-rule-is-now-in-full-effect-what-operators-need-to-know.html)
- Genshin order: under-16 purchase parental consent. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/rpg/ftc-says-genshin-impact-deceived-children-and-orders-its-publisher-to-pay-a-usd20-million-fine-and-stop-selling-loot-boxes-to-kids)
- Florida HB 3: under-14 ban, 14-15 parental consent; in effect after Oct 2026 Eleventh Circuit ruling. — [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/10/federal-appeals-court-finds-florida-social-media-ban-for-minors-likely-constitutional/)
- CAADCA (under-18) partially enforceable after March 2026. — [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/03/ninth-circuit-issues-mixed-ruling-on-california-age-appropriate-design-code-act)
- Texas app-store law in effect; Utah/LA/AL/CA laws by 2027 — they deliver age-category signals to developers. — [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/251111-texas-targets-app-stores-with-new-accountability-law); [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/comparing-enacted-app-store-accountability-acts/)
- Ofcom: enforce minimum ages with highly effective age checks. — [Ofcom](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/protecting-children/keep-underage-children-off-your-platforms-ofcom-tells-tech-firms)
- EU Art. 28 guidelines: self-declaration inappropriate; service open to minors without effective barriers is "accessible to minors." — [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/digital-services-act/european-commission-makes-new-announcements-on-the-protection-of-minors-under-the-digital-services-act/); [Danish Parliament doc](https://www.ft.dk/samling/20241/almdel/EUU/bilag/635/3053975/index.htm)
- Proposed DFA: under-18 bans on paid loot boxes/pay-to-win; paid currencies off-by-default where minors play with reasonable certainty. — [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/28479/european-commission-proposes-ban-on-paid-loot-boxes-and-pay-to-win-for-minors)

| Area | 13+ | 16+ | 18+ |
|---|---|---|---|
| COPPA | Out of scope if age gate neutral; must handle under-13 actual knowledge | Same | Same |
| CAADCA (CA, under-18) | In scope | In scope | Largely out if credibly enforced |
| App-store age-signal laws (TX now; UT/LA/AL/CA 2027) | Must branch for 13-15, 16-17; store parental consent | Must block/branch 13-15; handle 16-17 | Block all minor signals |
| State social-media consent (FL HB 3 etc.) | Possibly in scope if "social media" | Mostly out (HB 3 targets <16) | Out |
| FTC teen IAP expectations (Genshin) | Under-16 purchase consent | Lower risk | Lowest |
| CCPA minors' sale opt-in | Opt-in needed for 13-15 | Not needed (opt-out regime) | Not needed |
| UK OSA children's duties / ICO Children's Code | In scope | In scope (under-18) | Out only with highly effective age assurance |
| UK CAP "children" (<16) | In scope | Out | Out |
| EU DSA Art. 28 / DFA minors bans | In scope | In scope (under-18) | Out only with effective age verification |
| GDPR Art. 8 parental consent | Needed in 16-age countries for 13-15 | Generally not needed | Not needed |
| UGC voting design | Strict teen defaults | Teen defaults for 16-17 | Adult standards |

### Inferences
- 16+ is a "middle" that buys meaningful relief in US (CCPA opt-in, FL HB 3, FTC under-16 purchase consent) and UK advertising, but not in UK OSA/ICO or EU DSA, which bite at 18. For a UK/EU secondary launch, 16+ costs nearly as much as 13+ in safety-by-design work.
- 18+ is cheapest on design duties but requires investment in credible age assurance (store signals + estimation), and conflicts with the Gen Z (incl. 16-17) target audience.
- A pragmatic path: 18+ (or 17+ store rating) at launch in UK/EU; 16+ or 17+ in US with teen-safe defaults for any user flagged 16-17 by store signals; build teen mode later as a separate configuration (no affiliate tracking, no rewarded sharing, no data sale, purchase controls, avatar-only voting).
