# Product Data, Imagery Sourcing and IP/Legal Risk for a Real-Brand Fashion Styling Game (as of Nov 2026)

*Not legal advice. Items marked [LAWYER] need review by an IP or advertising attorney before launch.*

## 1. How existing styling products sourced brand items and images

### Takeaway
No major styling game got brand imagery just by scraping product photos. Each one ran on direct brand deals. Covet used free, unpaid "exposure" swaps with digitally rendered illustrations. Drest licensed luxury assets through Farfetch plus direct brand partners. Zepeto co-created virtual items with brands. Polyvore used retailer uploads plus user "clipping," and got sued for the clipping.

### Cited Findings
- **Covet Fashion (CrowdStar, now EA; launched Nov 12, 2013).** Brands don't pay to appear and CrowdStar doesn't pay them. Covet gets no fee on click-throughs. — [Fortune 2015](https://fortune.com/2015/09/09/covet-fashion-mobile-game/); [Wikipedia](https://www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Covet_Fashion)
- Covet's GM Sarah Fuchs (2019) said "There's no money exchanged." Brands get publicity and Covet gets to use their names. Covet partners only with brands, not retailers, and is positioned above H&M/Gap and below Hermès/Gucci. In 2019 it did not let users shop in-app. — [Glossy, Aug 29 2019](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/fashion-brands-are-linking-with-gaming-apps-to-acquire-customers)
- Sources conflict on shoppability. Other coverage says items link to places to buy them. — [search summary of Covet coverage, e.g. Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/move-kardashian-mobile-game-striking-chord-women-retailers)
- In Covet, players buy "digitally rendered illustrations" of each brand's in-season collection with virtual currency. Some drops are timed to NYFW debuts. French Connection's exclusive dress drove $12,000 in sales in 10 days and 110,000+ click-throughs. Reported brand counts range from about 150 to 175+ depending on year. — [Fortune 2015](https://fortune.com/2015/09/09/covet-fashion-mobile-game/)
- In 2017, Covet cross-promoted with The Zoe Report "Box of Style" using digitally rendered items. — [AList](https://alistdaily.com/digital/covet-fashion-zoe-report-box-style)
- In May 2025, a Covet x DRESSX capsule added items, a fashion pack and four branded Style Challenges. — [DRESSX](https://dressx.com/news/dressx-and-ea-mobile-s-covet-fashion-reveal-partnership-to-redefine-in-game-style); [EA](https://www.ea.com/games/covet-fashion/covet-fashion/news/step-into-the-future-with-new-brand-dressx)
- **Drest (Lucy Yeomans, ex-Harper's Bazaar UK/Porter).** It launched with 100+ luxury brands (Burberry, Gucci, Prada, Valentino), and a later count says 260+. Most brands came via Farfetch, which supplied product photos and fashion assets and handled transactions. Gucci was the first direct designer partnership. Cartier added a jewelry/watch mode. Drest raised £15M in June 2023 for Drest 2.0. — [FashionNetwork](https://us.fashionnetwork.com/news/New-styling-app-drest-combines-gaming-with-ecommerce,1144232.html); [TheIndustry.fashion](https://www.theindustry.fashion/drest-enters-the-hard-luxury-space-with-cartier-partnership/); [FashionNetwork 2023](https://ww.fashionnetwork.com/news/Drest-raises-more-funding-to-develop-metaverse-enabled-version-2-0,1529117.html)
- **Zepeto (Naver Z).** Brand deals produce co-created virtual items. Gucci (2021) supplied about 60 items plus a "Gucci Villa" world, with 800k+ items sold by Aug 2023 per Naver Z's own figures. Ralph Lauren (Aug 25 2021) supplied 12 looks and 50+ items. — [FashionUnited](https://fashionunited.com/news/fashion/gucci-partners-with-zepeto-to-personalise-avatars-and-virtual-world/2021020837944); [Ralph Lauren corporate](https://corporate.ralphlauren.com/pr_210825_ZepetoPartnership.html); [Naver press](https://www.navercorp.com/en/media/pressReleasesDetail?seq=31292)
- **Polyvore.** Retailers could upload their own product images and link back to product pages. Users could also "clip" images from any site. Polyvore was shut down after its acquisition by SSENSE in 2018. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyvore)
- In *BWP Media v. Polyvore*, a celebrity-photo agency sued over clipped images. In 2019 the 2nd Circuit partly revived the case. Polyvore's automated processes made extra copies of user images, which raised the question of whether Polyvore itself acted "volitionally," weakening its DMCA safe-harbor defense. — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2017/02/bwp-media-v-polyvore-its-all-about-control); [Copyright Alliance](https://copyrightalliance.org/copyright-law/copyright-cases/bwp-media-v-polyvore/); [Eric Goldman blog](https://blog.ericgoldman.org/?p=16191)
- **Lovelooks (2019).** Styles were photographed by Lovelooks or the brand and shown on a digital mannequin with affiliate "shop" buttons. Retailers could pay for featured mannequins. — [Glossy 2019](https://www.glossy.co/fashion/fashion-brands-are-linking-with-gaming-apps-to-acquire-customers)

### Inferences
- The proven route is to get **brand permission first** (free exposure swaps, as at Covet) and then render items in a consistent house art style. That avoids depending on licenses for product photos.
- An aggregator such as Farfetch for Drest, or a feed partner, can supply assets in bulk. Drest's deal was a commercial partnership, not ordinary affiliate terms.
- Polyvore shows that user-uploaded or scraped imagery creates copyright exposure, especially when the platform's automated processing copies the images.

### Gaps
- I found no public detail on Covet's in-house art pipeline (who draws items, time per item) or on the actual terms of Drest's Farfetch licence.
- The current state of Covet shoppability and affiliate links in 2026 is unverified.

## 2. Affiliate product feeds: what they provide and image-use terms

### Takeaway
Feeds (Awin/ShareASale, CJ, Rakuten, Impact) do supply image URLs, prices, availability and deep links. But the licence is typically narrow: use only to promote the advertiser's products, usually with **no modification**. Background removal, cutouts and placing items on an avatar probably count as modification under many merchant terms. A game would need written permission from each brand (or a network-level addendum) [LAWYER].

### Cited Findings
- Awin's standard publisher terms define "Advertiser Materials" to include images, and Awin's agreement prevails over publisher-supplied terms. — [Awin US Publisher Terms (PDF)](https://www.awin.com/docs.awin.com/Legal/Publisher+Terms/2023/US_EN+Awin+Ltd+Publisher+terms.pdf); [Awin publisher terms page](https://awin.com/publisher-terms)
- Awin's Storefront Addendum covers showing advertiser product images and information from feeds on publisher pages. It is a sign that storefront-style display is a specifically contracted use. — [Awin Storefront Addendum](https://www.awin.com/docs.awin.com/Legal/Awin-Storefront-Addendum-EN_GB.pdf)
- Merchant program terms on Awin say the following:
  - Lucy & Yak (US) allows only imagery/feed content supplied through Awin or approved in writing, and prohibits **modifying** logos, creatives or imagery. — [Awin Lucy & Yak US](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/127047/brand)
  - WJD Exclusives requires use of the official images and creatives provided in Awin. — [Awin WJD](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/82685/brand)
  - Ashley Jade bars modifying the appearance of its logos or brand names. — [Awin Ashley Jade](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/70264)
  - Pixum requires use of the brand name as given in its feed. — search summary of [Awin merchant terms](https://ui.awin.com/merchant-profile-terms/58823)
- **CJ.** Third-party feed vendors describe CJ feeds as carrying SKU, GTIN/EAN, price with currency, and in-stock/out-of-stock availability in XML. CJ also has a product-search API that returns names, descriptions, prices and tracking links. — [Pricefy](https://www.pricefy.io/feed-channels/cj-affiliate); [Koongo](https://www.koongo.com/channels/cj-com/). I could not retrieve CJ's own publisher policy text.
- **ShopMy.** It reports 50,000+ brand partners. A third-party API index lists Catalog, Links, Collections and Order Reporting APIs under OAuth or a developer key, which I could not confirm against official docs. — [ShopMy](https://shopmy.us/home/creators); [API Evangelist](https://providers.apievangelist.com/providers/shopmy/)
- **LTK.** I found no public product API. It has roughly 5,000–6,000 brand partners (secondary source), and retailers reportedly pay integration fees for catalog syndication. A Jan 2026 Puck piece reports that competition from ShopMy pushed LTK to "open up" data. — [Stack Influence](https://stackinfluence.com/blog/shopmy-vs-ltk-best-affiliate-platform-2026); [Everything PR](https://everything-pr.com/ltk-the-retail-affiliate-infrastructure-of-the-creator-economy); [Puck](https://puck.news/fashion/2026/1/affiliate-data-wars-how-shopmy-forced-ltk-to-open-up)
- **Google Merchant Center** is a merchant-side upload channel, not a publisher feed. Its specs (main image at least 500×500, about 1500×1500 recommended; no watermarks or promotional overlays) are what retailers already prepare. It enforces price and availability consistency between the feed and the landing page, and offers the Content/Merchant API for near-real-time updates. — [Google image_link](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6324350?hl=en); [Promotional overlays](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/6101131?hl=en); [Price mismatch](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12159029?hl=en); [Auto item updates](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12157888)

### Inferences
- Feed data (name, brand, price, availability, link, GTIN) is usable for the "real prices" mechanic under normal affiliate terms.
- Feed **images** are licensed for promotion "as is." Turning them into avatar-wearable cutouts is the riskiest step and needs written brand consent. A practical model is "affiliate for data and links, plus brand opt-in for wearable art," similar to Covet's exposure deal.
- Google Merchant data is not directly available to a third-party game. Its relevance is that retailers' feeds are already clean, with white or plain backgrounds and no watermarks.

### Gaps
- I couldn't extract the clause text of Awin's US terms (the PDF wasn't parseable). The actual CJ, Rakuten, Impact and Skimlinks image-licence language is unverified and needs a direct read [LAWYER].
- I found no network term that explicitly permits or forbids placing an image "on an avatar" or in a game.

## 3. Turning product photos into avatar-wearable 2D/3D assets: approaches and costs

### Takeaway
Options run from cheap 2D (background removal plus warping, or AI try-on at about $0.02–$0.10 per image) to hand illustration (no hard benchmarks found) to 3D CLO3D garments (about $39–$199+ per item from outsourcing studios, more for complex pieces). Brands that already have CLO3D files could hand them over, cutting cost.

### Cited Findings
- **AI virtual try-on API pricing (2026)** clusters around $0.04–$0.10 per image:
  - Google Vertex try-on is about $0.06 per image (via Picasso IA; Pixazo hosts it at $0.063).
  - Kling is about $0.07 via third parties.
  - fal.ai's endpoint is $0.04.
  - Pixelcut is $0.10.
  - Perfect Corp works out to about $0.096 per result.
  - FASHN's 4K mode is reportedly $0.375 per image, a figure from a competitor's comparison.
  - Fitroom claims $0.02 at 10K–20K volume, a self-published figure.
  - — [fal.ai 2026 review](https://fal.ai/learn/tools/best-virtual-try-on-apis-2026); [Perfect Corp](https://www.perfectcorp.com/business/blog/ai-clothes/virtual-try-on-api-pricing); [Pixazo](https://www.pixazo.ai/blog/introducing-google-virtual-try-on-api-on-pixazo-api); [Pixelcut](https://www.pixelcut.ai/api/try-on); [Fitroom](https://fitroom.app/blog/?p=1240)
- **CLO3D outsourcing:**
  - One studio lists custom 3D garments from $39, tech pack with 3D or ecommerce render sets from $149 per item, and AR/VR content from $199 per item.
  - A Contra freelancer offers $150 per style (front, back and side renders, two colorways, 3–5 business days).
  - Full-collection packages start at about $1,200 over three weeks.
  - CLO's own fabric digitization costs $60 per fabric plus $20 per extra color.
  - CLO software is about $50 per user per month (per a rival vendor).
  - Jackets, coats and multi-layer garments cost more. Existing DXF patterns reduce work.
  - — [BinaryCloth cost guide](https://binarycloth.com/clo3d-apparel-design-cost-guide/); [BinaryCloth services](https://binarycloth.com/service/); [Contra](https://contra.com/s/s23BNokW-3-d-garment-visualization-using-clo3-d); [CLO fabric service](https://www.clo3d.com:443/en/enterprise/fabric-service); [Style3D](https://www.style3d.com/blog/?p=9450)
- Google Merchant Center itself runs automatic image "improvements" that remove watermarks and overlays. Automated background and overlay removal is commodity tech. — [Google](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12724659)

### Inferences
- **2D cutout plus warp** onto a fixed-pose avatar is the cheapest per item, at cents with automated background removal plus QA. It is the most legally sensitive route because it modifies the brand's photo (see section 2).
- **Hand-drawn or house-style illustration** (Covet's "digitally rendered" items) creates new artwork. That reduces copyright reliance on the brand photo, but it is still a depiction of the brand's design and trademarks, so brand opt-in remains advisable.
- **AI try-on output** is a derivative of the input photo. Licensing of the source image still matters. US copyright protection for AI-generated output is also limited, so the game may not own strong rights in those assets [LAWYER].
- **3D** is only economical for hero or partner items, or where brands already supply CLO/Browzwear files.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable benchmark for freelance 2D fashion-illustration cost per item or for Covet's in-house throughput.
- I found no data on mismatch or QA rates when AI try-on is used on stylized avatars.

## 4. Legal risks (US focus, with UK/EU notes)

### Takeaway
The biggest risks are: (1) copyright in product photos when they are modified without a licence; (2) implied endorsement through brand names or logos, and especially celebrity names or likenesses in "recreate the look" challenges; (3) event marks (MET GALA, COACHELLA) that are actively enforced; (4) "dupe" framing that crosses into false advertising or implied affiliation. Truthful, minimal, nominative brand references are generally allowed, but this needs counsel review [LAWYER].

### Cited Findings
**Copyright in product photos**
- Product photos are copyrighted, and fashion companies sue over "stolen" product images. Fashion Nova named a rival in one such suit. — [TFL](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/fashion-nova-names-rival-in-copyright-lawsuit-over-stolen-product-images/)
- In Shein v. Temu (UK High Court trial, May 2026), Shein alleged "industrial-scale" copying of about 2,300 product images. Temu abandoned its defence on those images. Temu counterclaimed for damages, and the court upheld Temu's counterclaim over listings removed under injunctions on images Shein had no copyright in. — [TNW, May 11 2026](https://thenextweb.com/news/shein-temu-london-copyright-trial); [Law360 UK](https://www.law360.co.uk/articles/2476035); [Just Style](https://www.just-style.com/newsletters/shein-temu-clash-uk-court-copyright-claims)
- Polyvore was sued over user-clipped images (see section 1). — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2017/02/bwp-media-v-polyvore-its-all-about-control)

**Trademark, trade dress and expressive works**
- Comparative references that identify the product are generally permissible under *Smith v. Chanel*, if they don't suggest sponsorship. Brand-name use should be limited to what's reasonably necessary. — [Baker Botts, Oct 2026](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/october/lululemon-dupe)
- Nominative fair use permits using a competitor's mark when that is needed to convey information, without implying sponsorship. The standards vary by jurisdiction. — [Ropes & Gray, Jun 2025](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/06/imitation-game-legal-considerations-with-dupes-based-business-models)
- Trade dress claims require showing the design is non-functional and has acquired distinctiveness. Design patents use the "ordinary observer" test. — [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/october/lululemon-dupe)
- **Hermès v. Rothschild (MetaBirkins).** In Feb 2023 a jury found digital "MetaBirkin" NFTs infringed and diluted the Birkin mark. On appeal, the 2nd Circuit is weighing how *Jack Daniel's v. VIP* (2023) limits the Rogers artistic-expression defense when a mark is used as a source identifier. I could not confirm a final appellate decision. — [TFL](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/hermes-and-rothschild-metabirkins-fight-heads-to-the-second-circuit/); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/11/lessons-gleaned-from-the-metabirkin-case-on); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/metabirkin-nft-appeal-feels-jack-daniels-ruling-ripple-effect)
- Commentators expect the 2nd Circuit's *Vans* decision to apply: Rogers doesn't protect use that functions as a trademark. — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2024/11/lessons-gleaned-from-the-metabirkin-case-on)

**Right of publicity (celebrity looks)**
- The Fortnite emote suits (Ribeiro "Carlton," 2 Milly, Pellegrino) relied on right-of-publicity and false-endorsement claims. Courts applied the transformative-use test and dismissed most claims, but in Pellegrino a **false endorsement claim survived**. Names that allude to a celebrity raise the risk. — [Finnegan](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/blogs/incontestable/fortnite-sparks-a-battle-royale-over-the-right-to-use-dance-moves.html); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2020/04/gaming-emote-litigation-battle-royale-ensues-over); [FKKS](https://ipandmedialaw.fkks.com/post/102g4yb/epic-games-dances-around-infringement-claims-as-court-finds-transformative-use)
- Copyright in a celebrity outfit belongs to the designer or artist. In 2015, street artist Rime sued Moschino and Jeremy Scott over Katy Perry's Met Gala dress. Met Gala outfits get trademark protection only for non-functional, source-identifying designs. — [The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/news/brooklyn-street-artist-sues-over-katy-perry-s-graffitied-met-gala-gown); [Futurity](https://www.futurity.org/can-you-copyright-a-met-gala-outfit-3279382/)

**Event names**
- The Metropolitan Museum of Art filed a MET GALA application in 2020 for event services. A 2019 third-party MET GALA filing was abandoned after an inter partes decision. Current registration status is unverified. — [Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/885/96/met-88596707.html); [Justia Met owner](https://trademarks.justia.com/owners/the-metropolitan-museum-of-art-1886770)
- Coachella and Goldenvoice sued Urban Outfitters/Free People in 2017 over unlicensed "Coachella" apparel listings; the case settled and was dismissed Sept 29, 2017. Coachella also won a preliminary injunction and then a permanent injunction against "Filmchella" (2017–18). — [WIPR](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/coachella-settles-trademark-clash-with-urban-outfitters-14686); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/news/general-news/urban-outfitters-sued-by-coachella-unlicensed-coachella-apparel-986874/); [Fox Rothschild](https://advertisinglaw.foxrothschild.com/2018/10/coachella-filmchella-dispute-ends/)

**"Dupe" marketing**
- **Lululemon v. Costco** (C.D. Cal., filed June 2025) targeted the $20 Hi-Tec Scuba dupe and others. Claims were trade dress, design patent, trademark, Lanham Act unfair competition and California UCL. It partially settled in March 2026 (Danskin/Jockey items), and all claims except one men's jacket settled by May 8, 2026. Baker Botts says the case ended in August 2026 on undisclosed terms with no ruling on the merits. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/lululemon-costco-settle-dupes-lawsuit-except-for-one-jacket); [Bloomberg Law partial](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/lululemon-costco-partly-settle-ip-lawsuit-over-dupe-jackets); [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/october/lululemon-dupe); [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2025/07/01/nx-s1-5453696/lululemon-costco-lawsuit-fashion-knockoffs-dupes)
- Lululemon registered **LULULEMON DUPE** (Reg. 7,990,938, Oct 21 2025, Class 35) and **DUPE SWAP** (Reg. 7,908,805, Aug 19 2025). Heavy brand use in dupe marketing could support an affiliation argument. — [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2026/october/lululemon-dupe)
- The trend is that brands are adding Lanham Act **false advertising** claims against comparative "dupe" charts and language. Examples: Williams-Sonoma v. Quince ("Beyond Compare" charts) and Sol de Janeiro v. MCoBeauty ("smells exactly like," with a puffery defense). Louis Vuitton sued United Monograms over "designer dupe" goods. — [TFL](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/false-advertising-comes-under-fire-in-new-dupe-lawsuits/); [TFL Sol de Janeiro](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/sol-de-janeiro-dupes-lawsuit-faces-pushback-from-mcobeauty/); [TFL LV](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/louis-vuitton-sues-united-monograms-over-designer-dupe-wares/); [Fashion Law Journal](https://fashionlawjournal.com/what-is-a-dupe-when-dupes-break-the-law/)

**UK/EU differences**
- The UK has **no free-standing image or publicity right**. Celebrities rely on **passing off**, which needs goodwill and a false impression of endorsement. In *Fenty v Arcadia* (Rihanna v Topshop, [2013] EWHC 2310; upheld [2015] EWCA Civ 3), the court said using an image is not in itself wrongful, but implied authorization is. See also *Irvine v Talksport* (2002) on doctored endorsement. — [National Archives](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2015/3); [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/media-and-ip/victory-for-rihanna-against-topshop-s-use-of-her-image/); [WTR](https://www.worldtrademarkreview.com/article/rihanna-successfully-sues-topshop-passing-over-t-shirt-bearing-her-photograph)
- UK copyright litigation over product photos is active (Shein v Temu above). — [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/shein-temu-london-copyright-trial)

### Inferences
- For a "recreate Zendaya's Met Gala look" challenge, the risky elements are celebrity name or photo plus event mark plus the designer's original artwork. Safer framing is generic or descriptive, for example "red-carpet gala look" or "desert festival look." Avoid celebrity images, and use names only in a clearly editorial or descriptive way, with no implied endorsement [LAWYER].
- For "dupe" challenges, players assemble mid-market items to *evoke* a look. Safer than calling items "dupes of [Brand]": avoid brand-versus-brand price or quality claims and avoid showing designer logos. Calling a mid-market partner's item a "[Designer] dupe" could also irritate that partner, and designers have shown they will sue.
- Brand names on real items, with the brand's consent through partnership or feed terms, are low risk. Logos rendered on garments need consent. Avoid inventing branded items.
- In the US, publicity law is state-by-state (e.g., CA and NY statutes), so a lawyer should assess multi-state exposure. The EU varies by member state.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the final 2nd Circuit MetaBirkins outcome, the current MET GALA registration status, the USPTO status of COACHELLA marks, or any 2026 case law on AI-generated likeness and digital replicas (e.g., state "digital replica" laws).
- EU dupe and comparative advertising rules (Comparative Advertising Directive) were not researched in depth.

## 5. Keeping inventory and prices fresh

### Takeaway
Feeds carry price and availability fields. Refresh cadence ranges from 24 hours on basic plans to near real time. Use a daily-or-better sync, delta updates, and graceful handling of out-of-stock items, such as keeping the item wearable in the game but showing it as "sold out" or swapping in a similar item.

### Cited Findings
- CJ feed fields include current price with currency and in/out-of-stock availability. Vendors recommend daily or intraday updates. Koongo syncs every 30, 15 or 5 minutes by plan. — [Pricefy](https://www.pricefy.io/feed-channels/cj-affiliate); [Koongo](https://www.koongo.com/channels/cj-com/)
- A Shopify app pushing to Awin/CJ updates every 24 hours on its basic plan and in real time on its top plan. Merchant-side refresh rates therefore vary by advertiser. — [Shopify app](https://apps.shopify.com/dpl-product-feed-inventory)
- Awin offers a retail product API. — [Awin API docs](https://help.awin.com/apidocs/retail-advertiser-productapidocumentation)
- Google flags price and availability mismatches between feed and landing page. Causes include caching and variant confusion. Google recommends the Merchant/Content API for near-real-time updates. — [Google](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12159029?hl=en); [Google auto-updates](https://support.google.com/merchants/answer/12157888)

### Inferences
- Show prices as "price at last check" with a timestamp to limit consumer-protection and false-advertising exposure from stale prices [LAWYER].
- Design the game so that sold-out items stay as collectible art. That decouples game content from inventory churn.

### Gaps
- I found no official documentation for refresh limits or rate limits on Rakuten, Impact or Skimlinks.

## 6. Fast-fashion (Shein/Temu) IP controversies relevant to partnering

### Takeaway
Shein and Temu are entangled in repeated IP litigation over copied designs and product photos. Partnering with them risks reputational spillover, and also the risk of hosting infringing designs or images in the game.

### Cited Findings
- In 2023, designers Krista Perry, Larissa Martinez and Jay Baron filed a US federal RICO suit alleging Shein's algorithm-driven "systemic" copyright infringement. When Perry complained, Shein reportedly offered her $500. — [TIME](https://time.com/6295035/shein-lawsuit-copyright-infringement); [CBS](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/shein-lawsuit-rico-violations/); [WIPR](https://www.worldipreview.com/news/shein-used-algorithms-in-systematic-ip-theft-artists-claim-24083)
- A 2024 proposed class action covers US copyright owners whose works were used in Shein products, and alleges AI and scraping. — [Fortune 2024](https://dc.fortune.com/2024/04/16/shein-class-action-lawsuit-copyright-infringement-data-scraping-ai-art-alan-giana-china); [ClassAction.org](https://classaction.org/blog/shein-lawsuit-alleges-e-commerce-giant-uses-ai-to-steal-sell-copyrighted-designs)
- Shein sued Temu over copyright (US, 2024). ### Inferences
- Marketplace-model retailers (Temu, and Shein's marketplace) have third-party sellers whose image rights are uncertain. Even feed-supplied images may not be properly licensed, which raises the game's risk if it reuses them. Mid-market brands with in-house photography and clear feed terms are safer partners.
- An IP-indemnity clause should be required in any brand or feed partnership [LAWYER].
