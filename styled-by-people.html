Investment case · Concept stage · November 2026

AI can style you. Only people can tell you it works.

A styling game where players dress real outfits from affordable brands at real prices, for real-life briefs, and other players vote on the best look. Every look can be saved and shopped.

The problem

Gen Z uses AI every day but does not trust it to judge an outfit

The AI styling race is well funded. Alta raised $20M in October 2026 and has generated 700M outfits. Daydream raised a $50M seed. Google, Amazon, OpenAI and Pinterest all have AI shopping tools.

All of them generate looks. None lets real people judge them. Yet the people Gen Z trusts most are people: friends at 34% and family at 27%, against 2% for AI.

The gap is a trusted human verdict. Player voting fills it, and every look on the platform is made by a person, which makes it a library of human-approved outfits anyone can save and shop.

The need

Styling help is a mass need, and the paid options are priced for a few

The product

A daily styling challenge with real clothes, real prices and real judges

Get a brief

"First job interview, wants to look taller, $150 budget." Daily, weekly and seasonal briefs.

Style it

Dress the model from real affordable brands. A $45 top costs 45 coins.

Submit

Every submission earns coins, so players can keep playing for free.

Get voted on

Other players vote head-to-head. Winners earn prizes, rank and brand unlocks.

Save and shop

Any look can be saved, and every item links to the real product.

Briefs built on real bodies

The client's height, shape and goals are part of the challenge. Players learn the most useful real-world styling skill without it being about their own body.

Teaches money sense

Real prices and limited wears per item teach budgeting and cost per wear. A $60 pair of jeans worn 20 times beats a $25 top worn twice.

Fair by design

No paid vote boosts, ever. Head-to-head pairs, more weight for trusted voters and no downvotes address the genre's top complaint.

Market size

3.5M likely players in the US alone, and a $0.6–13.5M business within reach

The two lenses agree. Bottom-up, the likely players are worth $21–189M a year. Top-down, today's styling games already earn about $130M, mostly with fantasy items and no real-world shopping. Capturing 3–7% of the likely players puts the business at Covet's scale today.

Why now

Six shifts make 2027 a good moment to start

The AI wave left a gapEvery funded player builds AI that generates looks. Human judgment is the missing layer, and only 2% of young people trust AI here.
The incumbents stepped backCovet's revenue has fallen sharply since 2019 and Drest moved to luxury sponsorships. Nobody holds the mid-market lane.
Gen Z is trading down58% of shoppers have bought a dupe, and buyers now want quality too. "Affordable and good" is the brief.
App store fees fellSince April 2026, US iOS apps can send players to web checkout with no Apple fee, and Google's US rate dropped to 20%. (A court may still set a fee for Apple.)
Adults play styling gamesAdults are 37% of US Roblox daily users and growing 18% a year. Dress to Impress drew 10.4B visits with no paid ads.
Small brands want a firstPrincess Polly, Oh Polly, Edikted and Good American have never done a game deal. Being their first playable catalog is a real pitch.
Go-to-market

Prove demand before building, with clear go and no-go gates

Phase 1 · Weeks 1–8

Test demand with content

  • TikTok and Instagram account running "the look for under $150" challenges
  • Followers submit looks; the community votes in polls and comments
  • One wave of 10 micro-creators (midsize, petite, plus-size, budget)
  • Pitch 10 small brands on a free "exposure for items" swap
Go if~1,000 engaged followers, 50+ submissions per challenge, 3 interested brands
Phase 2 · Weeks 8–16

Test the game on mobile web

  • Simple web prototype: collage board, daily brief, coins at real prices, head-to-head voting
  • 200 players invited from the content audience
  • Test a $4.99 coin pack and shoppable clicks
Go ifDay-30 retention near 4% (top 10% of games; the median is under 1%)
Phase 3 · Month 5 onward

Build the real thing

  • Game-design cofounder or lead hire
  • Legal review of age rules, brand permissions and naming
  • Native app with avatar, launched 18+ in the US
  • Pre-seed raise backed by real traction

The risks are known, and each one has a plan

Players don't come backHighGate every build step on retention; daily briefs, seasonal events and rank progression

Brand permission to show itemsMediumCovet's model: free exposure swaps with written opt-in; product feeds only for prices and links
Event names and "dupe" wordingMedium"Desert festival," "Gala night," "The look for under $150"; no celebrity or designer names
Age rulesMediumLaunch 18+ (17+ store rating), US first; build a teen mode later as a separate setup
A big player copies itMediumThe moat is the voting community, voter reputation, brand rights and data on which looks win
